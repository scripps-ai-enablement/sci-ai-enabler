## MAP2K1/MAP2K2 (MEK1/MEK2) — Working Dossier

**Assumption made:** You didn't specify organism/isoform/cancer context, so this covers human **MAP2K1 (MEK1, UniProt Q02750)** as primary, noting where **MAP2K2 (MEK2)** diverges functionally. If your hit is actually a specific mutant/cell-line context, the mechanism section below tells you which class it likely falls into.

### 1. Disease links & mechanism
MEK1/2 are dual-specificity Ser/Thr+Tyr kinases sitting immediately downstream of RAF in the canonical **RAS→RAF→MEK→ERK** cascade; ERK1/2 are their *only* known physiological substrates, and the pathway is dysregulated in >30% of human cancers ([Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41392-023-01705-z), [Nat Rev Cancer](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrc4000)).
- **Somatic cancer driver:** Recurrent activating *MAP2K1* mutations occur in melanoma (a subset defined by in-frame deletions, mutually exclusive with BRAF/NRAS/NF1), lung, colorectal, and ovarian cancers ([PMC7685971](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7685971/), [PMC9890140](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9890140/)). Mutations sort into three functional classes: **Class 1** (still RAF-phosphorylation-dependent), **Class 2** (constitutively active, RAF-independent but further boostable), **Class 3** (fully RAF-independent, and structurally distorted enough to block clinical MEK-inhibitor binding).
- **Germline/RASopathy:** Heterozygous gain-of-function *MAP2K1*/*MAP2K2* germline mutations cause **cardiofaciocutaneous (CFC) syndrome** (~25% of mutation-positive CFC cases; BRAF accounts for ~75%), with craniofacial dysmorphism, congenital heart defects, growth failure, and skin/hair anomalies ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/cardiofaciocutaneous-syndrome/), [PMC9614356](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9614356/)).
- **Resistance mechanism:** MAP2K1 point mutations at the inhibitor-binding interface are a recurrent mechanism of acquired resistance to BRAF/MEK inhibitor therapy; MEK2^C125S (not the analogous MEK1^C121S) specifically drives combination-therapy resistance in melanoma ([Cell Reports](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247(13)00464-6)).

### 2. Protein domains & PTMs
- **Architecture:** N-terminal negative-regulatory/docking region containing an ERK-docking (D-)domain, a central **protein kinase domain (~residues 68–381)** with the activation loop, and a C-terminal proline-rich domain (PRD) ([GeneCards](https://www.genecards.org/cgi-bin/carddisp.pl?gene=MAP2K1)). Kinase-family classification: STE group, MAP2K subfamily.
- **Key activating PTMs:** RAF (chiefly BRAF) phosphorylates the activation-loop residues **Ser218 and Ser222**; this is the on-switch, and mutations here abolish MEK/ERK activation ([PMC9890140](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9890140/); antibody-validated in [Abcam](https://www.abcam.com/en-us/targets/map2k1-phospho-s218/418351)).
- **Feedback/regulatory PTMs:** ERK2 phosphorylates **Thr292** (negative feedback), and **PAK** phosphorylates **Ser298** (modulates RAF coupling).
- **Structural note:** Substitutions/deletions near this activation-loop and helix-C interface are exactly what produce Class 2/3 oncogenic mutants, so if your hit maps near S218/S222 or the adjacent helix-C, that's mechanistically consistent with constitutive activation.

### 3. Subcellular localization
Predominantly **cytoplasmic** at steady state, with regulated nucleocytoplasmic shuttling. A leucine-rich **nuclear export signal (NES) at residues ~32–51** actively keeps MEK1 (and, via piggy-back transport, ERK) out of the nucleus; this NES is the mechanistic basis for cytoplasmic retention, and its disruption allows nuclear translocation upon mitogenic stimulation ([JBC](https://www.jbc.org/article/S0021-9258(18)98294-3/fulltext), [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.94.8.3742)). Also reported at focal adhesions, axons/dendrites in neuronal contexts ([Human Protein Atlas](https://www.proteinatlas.org/ENSG00000169032-MAP2K1)).

### 6. Drug sensitivity
MEK1/2 are clinically druggable via **allosteric (non-ATP-competitive) inhibitors**: trametinib, selumetinib, cobimetinib, binimetinib — all FDA-approved or late-stage, mostly paired with BRAF inhibitors in BRAF^V600-mutant melanoma ([Nat Rev Cancer](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrc4000)).
- Trametinib monotherapy: ~25% confirmed response rate, ~4-month PFS in BRAF-mutant metastatic melanoma ([JCO](https://ascopubs.org/doi/abs/10.1200/JCO.2012.43.5966)).
- In *MAP2K1*-mutated tumors specifically, binimetinib gave a **42.8% objective response rate** ([JCO Precision Oncology](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/PO.24.00199)) — but **Class 3 MAP2K1 mutants structurally block binding of all three approved MEK inhibitors**, so mutation class matters enormously for predicted drug sensitivity.
- Resistance recurrently arises via second-site MAP2K1/2 mutations at the inhibitor pocket, or BRAF/RAS amplification restoring pathway flux ([Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-09438-w)).

### Bottom line
If your "hit" is wild-type MEK1/2 showing up in a CRISPR screen, that's expected — it's a core, broadly-essential pathway node, so essentiality alone doesn't tell you much about selectivity; look instead at whether specific lineages/genotypes (RAS/RAF-mutant) show *outsized* dependency relative to the common-essential baseline. If it's a variant/mutant hit, the highest-value next step is classifying it into Class 1/2/3 (per the functional-classification framework above) since that single call predicts both oncogenicity and whether existing MEK inhibitors will even bind it.
