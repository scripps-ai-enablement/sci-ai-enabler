## Scope & key assumption

You didn't specify how strictly to define "approved compound," so I'm using the broadest defensible reading: any drug/biologic with an existing FDA/EMA approval (for any indication), evaluated either as a direct KRAS‑pathway agent or as a mechanistically-linked combination partner, with actual PDAC clinical or strong preclinical data. I've included the two approved KRAS G12C inhibitors themselves (approved for NSCLC, not PDAC) since they are the most direct "repurposing into a new indication" case.

## 1. Disease-target genetic & expression rationale (must state up front)

- KRAS is mutated in ~90–92% of PDAC, but **G12C is rare within that population — only ~1.7–2%** of PDAC cases, versus G12D (~40%), G12V (~29%), and G12R (~15%) ([PMC11911800](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11911800/); [JCO abstract, FoundationMedicine real-world data](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2025.43.4_suppl.777); [PMC8380752](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8380752/)). Any G12C-directed shortlist is therefore a **biomarker-selected niche within PDAC**, not a pan-KRAS-mutant strategy — screen assumes molecular confirmation of G12C by NGS before enrollment.
- Downstream pathway dependency is not rare, however: essentially all KRAS-mutant PDAC shows constitutive MAPK (RAF‑MEK‑ERK) and PI3K‑AKT‑mTOR activation regardless of allele, which is why pathway-node drugs (MEK, EGFR, FAK, CDK4/6) have broader rationale than G12C-selective inhibitors ([targeted KRAS review, PMC11055996](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11055996/)).
- Genomic co-alterations relevant to interpreting expression/dependency data: CDKN2A, TP53, and SMAD4 co-loss is near-universal and shapes resistance biology (cell-cycle bypass via CDK4/6, apoptotic threshold), which is why CDK4/6 and MEK co-targeting keep recurring in the mechanistic literature.
- Adaptive resistance to any KRAS G12C blockade in PDAC is driven substantially by EGFR/RTK reactivation — this is the specific mechanistic bridge that justifies combining G12C inhibitors with EGFR-targeted approved agents ([rationale discussed alongside CRC data, PMC11535810](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11535810/)).

## 3. Reconciled read: what this evidence actually supports

- **Highest direct-mechanism confidence:** Adagrasib and sotorasib — real G12C-mutant PDAC response data (ORR 21–33%), but applicable to only the ~2% G12C-positive PDAC slice, and both carry a meaningful hepatotoxicity signal requiring LFT monitoring.
- **Best-supported combination strategy:** adding an approved anti-EGFR agent (panitumumab/cetuximab/erlotinib) to a G12C inhibitor — validated in CRC, mechanistically transposable to PDAC, not yet PDAC-trial-confirmed.
- **Most promising non-G12C-selective pathway play:** avutometinib+defactinib — the only agent combination with both a fresh FDA approval anchored in KRAS-mutant biology and an 83% ORR PDAC signal (RAMP 205, early/small n).
- **Mechanistically attractive but clinically disproven as monotherapy:** simvastatin and trametinib — both failed their respective randomized PDAC trials; keep in the screen only as combination partners (e.g., trametinib+HCQ), not standalone bets.
- **Weak/inconclusive but very low-risk repurposing candidates:** metformin, hydroxychloroquine, disulfiram, niclosamide — good safety margins and mechanistic rationale, but none has PDAC survival data strong enough to prioritize alone; best used as adjuncts in combination-arm design.

**Caveat:** all trial-derived numbers above reflect the specific cohorts/doses cited in each source — verify current ClinicalTrials.gov status before designing new protocols, since several of these programs (avutometinib/defactinib PDAC indication, RECAP ruxolitinib subgroup) are still evolving.
