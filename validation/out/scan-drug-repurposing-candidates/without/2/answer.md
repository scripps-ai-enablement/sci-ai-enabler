## Assumptions (single-shot, no follow-up)
Since you can't clarify: I'm assuming (1) **advanced/metastatic, chemo-refractory PDAC** (the setting of all real trial data below), (2) "approved compounds" means FDA/EMA-approved drugs — for *any* indication — being considered for repurposing, not investigational-only KRAS inhibitors, and (3) you want both **direct KRAS G12C-mutant PDAC** candidates and **pathway-node** candidates relevant to KRAS-driven PDAC broadly, since I flag below that G12C-mutant PDAC is a small slice of the disease.

## Genetic/expression evidence — the key caveat up front

KRAS mutations occur in **~90–92% of PDAC** overall, but the allelic spectrum is dominated by G12D (~40%) and G12V (~29%), with G12R ~15%; **KRAS G12C is present in only ~1–3% of PDAC** ([Foundation Medicine real-world data, JCO 2025](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2025.43.4_suppl.777); [PMC11012482](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11012482/)) — a rate an order of magnitude lower than in NSCLC (~13%). Practically: a *G12C-selective* drug only ever applies to a small, biomarker-defined PDAC subset, so a repurposing shortlist worth testing has to include drugs active at **other nodes of the same oncogenic pathway** (RAF‑MEK‑ERK, EGFR-driven adaptive resistance, PI3K‑AKT‑mTOR, autophagy, and mevalonate/prenylation), which apply across the much larger KRAS‑G12D/V/R population and have PDAC‑specific transcriptomic support (basal‑like/quasi‑mesenchymal PDAC subtypes show elevated MAPK output and worse prognosis; Moffitt/Collisson subtyping). I've tiered the table accordingly — Tier 1 rows act directly on G12C, Tier 2 rows act on pathway members with PDAC-relevant genetic/expression rationale and real trial exposure.

## How I'd prioritize for an actual screen
- **Tier 1 (direct, biomarker-gated):** adagrasib > sotorasib, paired with cetuximab/panitumumab or erlotinib — but only actionable in the ~1–3% G12C-mutant PDAC subset, so a companion diagnostic gate is mandatory before dosing.
- **Tier 2 (pathway, broad KRAS-mutant PDAC):** trametinib/binimetinib + hydroxychloroquine is the most mechanistically and clinically mature combination (active trials, clear resistance rationale); metformin + statin (pravastatin preferred over simvastatin for DDI reasons) is the lowest-risk, lowest-cost adjunct worth layering onto any backbone given consistent epidemiologic signal.
- **Deprioritize/negative controls:** sorafenib, regorafenib, and single-agent MEK inhibition (trametinib or selumetinib alone) — real trial data already argues against these as monotherapy in PDAC; keep them in the screen only as combination or comparator arms, not standalone bets.
- **Explicitly excluded** from "approved" status but worth tracking for future repurposing once approved: SHP2 inhibitors (RMC-4630, TNO155) and SOS1 inhibitors — mechanistically the most rational G12C-inhibitor combination partners (blocking RAS-GTP reloading), but still investigational-only as of mid-2026, so out of scope for an approved-compound shortlist.
